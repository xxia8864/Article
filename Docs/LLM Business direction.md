# The Real Gap for LLMs Isn’t Benchmarks — It’s Everyday Value

*Note: This article is AI assisted. As a non-native English speaker, this is exactly the kind of capability barrier LLMs solve.*

## Background
OpenAI recently declared an internal “code red,” shifting resources toward core model improvements and away from several user-facing initiatives.

In my view, the central challenge is not benchmark performance.

It is that most ordinary users still struggle to derive **clear, recurring, tangible value** from the technology.

## Current models are already capable enough
Today's models pass the bar exam and solve graduate math problems — they're already beyond what most people need. By most practical measures, they exceed human performance on a wide range of difficult tasks.

Further gains on leaderboards are real technical achievements, but they remain invisible to the average user.

## Scale without monetization
ChatGPT reached over 700 million weekly active users in August 2025 — the fastest consumer adoption of any technology in history.

Yet only a small fraction subscribe.

Most people try it a few times, find it interesting, and then stop using it regularly, because they found LLM provides no real value.

## What actually matters to ordinary users

### Capability barriers

Many people have ideas but lack the specific capabilities needed to execute them.

LLMs directly remove this barrier.

As a non-native English speaker, I used LLM to help turn my ideas into a clear, grammatically correct article. The ideas themselves are mine.

Here is another example:

I recently had an aging water line that needed replacement.

Plumbers quoted wildly different prices, and I had no way to judge what was reasonable.

I asked ChatGPT, and it gave me the exact steps and tools required.

It even helped me troubleshoot during the process and get the job done.

### information asymmetry 

Last month I needed a 32-inch monitor. Simple requirements: HDMI + DP + USB-C with power delivery, no dongles and decent color accuracy. 

The market has hundreds of models. Comparing spec sheets manually is impossible. Reviews are scattered and contradictory. 

I spent 30 minutes with ChatGPT, feeding it specs and reviews. It found exactly one model meeting every requirement with no compromises. 

First time in years an AI shopping assistant actually saved me time and money.

The same pattern appears everywhere:
- Information is hard to obtain and synthesize
- High cost of error (hundreds to tens of thousands of dollars, or even injury or legal consequences)
- Professional advice is costly and not scalable

Until now, no tool has combined:

1. Comprehensive real-world data access
2. The ability to accurately understand detailed, 
   personalized requirements

Below are some examples:
- Selecting a TV, air purifier, stroller, or water filter from thousands of similar products and conflicting reviews
- Renting or buying a home while evaluating school districts, flood risk, HOA rules, and price history
- Choosing schools, tutors, or extracurricular programs amid fake reviews and changing admission policies
- Planning international travel with multiple flights, visas, cancellation rules, and local risks
Negotiating salary, interpreting equity offers, or understanding labor-law rights

In each case:
- Search engines and vertical sites (Google, Zillow, Yelp, etc.) have the data but cannot interpret complex personal constraints.
- Friends, Reddit threads, and TikTok/YouTube reviews partly understand the user but rely on tiny, biased samples.
- Professional advisors and agents understand both sides but charge fees that would cover years of AI subscriptions.

LLMs are the first tool able to ingest all available data, align unambiguously with the user’s interests, and carry on a coherent conversation about specific needs.

## Conclusion
Improving a model from 95 to 99 points on artificial benchmarks adds almost no perceived value for ordinary users.

Helping them avoid one mistake that costs several thousand dollars — or find one option that saves several thousand dollars — changes perceived value immediately.

The technical capability already exists.

The remaining question is whether companies choose to apply it to the decisions ordinary people actually face.
